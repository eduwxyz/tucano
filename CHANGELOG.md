# Changelog

What changed in each version of Tucano. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org) (while in 0.x, a minor version may change things). Each section is also the release's notes on GitHub, which is what the app's "What's new" link opens.

## [Unreleased]

> **Em português:**
> - **Novo:** **oriente uma resposta em curso** pela linha dos passos dela. Enquanto um agente trabalha, um botão compacto **Orientar** fica ao lado de **Parar**; ele abre um campo sob a caixa dos passos, e o que você escreve vai para aquela resposta no próximo chamado de ferramenta dela, com Ctrl+Enter ou o botão, e Esc fecha sem enviar nada. Uma orientação não é um prompt: nada dela é publicado no canal ou na DM, e ela aparece na cadeia da resposta como uma linha própria, com as suas palavras, que passa de em curso para lida quando a resposta a recebe no chamado seguinte. Uma resposta que termina antes de ler deixa aquela linha na cadeia, marcada como não lida, em vez de virar uma mensagem. Com nada em curso ali, o mesmo texto é um prompt comum, e a pílula some com o trabalho.
> - **Corrigido:** o campo de uma orientação abre na largura da cadeia, de onde o texto dos passos começa até onde ela termina, em janela larga e estreita, sem rolagem lateral.
> - **Novo:** veja **o que o modelo pensa** enquanto responde. Ligue em Configurações › Você › Conversas › **O que o modelo pensa**: o raciocínio aparece ao vivo em letra pequena e apagada embaixo dos passos e, quando a resposta acaba, fica dobrado numa linha **Pensou** que um clique abre. Vem desligado, e é de cada pessoa. Por enquanto só os agentes que rodam no Pi mandam o raciocínio.
> - **Novo:** um prompt novo numa conversa que já está respondendo ganha uma resposta própria, ao lado das que estão em curso, até o limite de **Configurações › Agentes › Respostas** (uma por vez, até 2, 3, 4 ou 6, o padrão). Passando do limite, ele espera na fila, diz que está esperando, e continua na fila mesmo se o app fechar. Na página de um agente, **Uma resposta por vez** faz o próximo prompt para ele esperar a resposta em curso, em qualquer conversa. O Enter só envia: orientar uma resposta é pelo botão dela, e parar é pelo **Parar**. Rotinas, **Tentar de novo** e **Continuar** também rodam ao lado da resposta que já está ali, em vez de esperar.

### Added

- **A new prompt runs beside the replies already working, up to a limit.** A second message to an agent that was still answering used to wait for that reply to end, folded into one answer with the others. Now it starts its own reply beside the ones running in that conversation, up to the limit in **Settings › Agents › Replies** (one at a time, up to 2, 3, 4, or 6, the default). Past the limit it waits in the queue, says so on the message, and survives a restart. On an agent's page, **One reply at a time** makes the next prompt for that agent wait for the running reply, in any conversation, while other agents still go in parallel. Enter only sends: a reply is steered from its own pill and stopped with Stop. A routine, a retry and a reply going on after its budget pause also start beside the reply running there. Two replies of one agent in one place share its session, so the second one reads the whole conversation instead. Thanks to Apostol Apostolov. (#261)
- **Steering a reply that is still working.** A reply at work could be stopped, but not directed: what to do next had to be a message of its own, answered after it finished. Now a compact **Orientar** (steer) sub-button sits beside Stop in the steps row of a working reply; it opens a field under the box, and what the person writes reaches that reply at its next tool call, sent with Ctrl+Enter or the button, and Esc closes it without sending. A steer is not a prompt: nothing of it is posted in the channel or the DM, and it becomes a row of that reply's chain in their words, marked as taken when the reply reads it at the following call. A reply that ends before reading one leaves its row in the chain, marked as not read, instead of turning into a message. With nothing running in that place, the same text is an ordinary prompt. The steer carries the reply's id, so a second reply of the same agent in a DM isn't steered by what was written for the first. Agents read it with the tool result that follows, said in so many words as what the person wrote while they worked.
- **See what the model is thinking.** Turn on Settings › You › Conversations › **What the model is thinking**, and a reply's reasoning streams in small, dim text under its steps while it works; once the reply ends it folds into a **Thought** line that opens on a click. A long thought keeps the place you're reading while the rest streams in. It's off by default and each person's own. The reasoning stays in the window's memory, not in the database, so it's gone after a restart. For now only agents running on Pi send it. Thanks to Apostol Apostolov.
- **Sort the whole sidebar by hand: hold a row, see where it lands, or move it with the keyboard.** Press and hold a row for a moment to pick it up: it dims, and an orange line shows where it will land as you move. Let go there and the order is saved; let go where it was and it just opens, like a click. It works for the items at the top (Aprovações, Quadro, Conhecimento, Operação; the Tuca stays first), the channels (let go on a group's name to put a channel in that group), the agents, and the sectors. An agent moves only within its sector. With the keyboard, **Alt+↑** and **Alt+↓** move the row you're on. Before, only channels could be moved, by dragging. An item you took out of the sidebar stays out, and keeps its place for when it comes back. In a team, the order is yours alone, like the channel groups. Thanks to Apostol Apostolov.

### Changed

- **Clearer English interface copy.** Settings, Memory, routines, Vault and related dialogs use explicit actions and consequences, with shorter hints and clearer recovery messages. Portuguese wording and application behavior are unchanged.
- **Settings are organized in five groups, with a search.** The eight tabs became five groups at the top of the page: **You** (General, Appearance, Notifications, Dictation, Conversations), **Agents** (Autonomy, Limits, Routines, Tuca, Browser), **Models** (Agent models, Harnesses, Accounts, Router, Memory), **Connections** (Phone and Telegram, Servers, MCP access) and **Workspace** (People, Activity, Move the team, Backups, Updates, Data and deletion). Each subject has its own coloured tile and name, its controls sit in rounded groups of rows, and a row of links under the groups goes straight to a subject. The search at the top right finds a control by what you'd call it ("quiet hours", "limit", "sounds") and opens its group right at it. Appearance, with the theme, is there for everyone now, not only for members. A member sees only the groups they have something in. No setting, value, default or permission changes, and a link to an old tab (Settings › Servers, Settings › People) still lands on its subject. `npm run check:settings` holds the declaration, the subjects and the groups to each other. Thanks to Apostol Apostolov.
- **A routine that's off keeps its place in the week.** Turning a routine off made it leave the timetable, so the week stopped showing when it would run and there was nothing there to turn back on. It now stays at its times, dimmed and in monochrome, and its own panel still turns it back on; only a routine that was deleted leaves the timetable.
- **In a DM, the gear at the top right opens the agent's settings.** It opened a "Conversation with …" window that had nothing in it. It now takes you to the agent's page, where its model, instructions and tools are. Thanks to Apostol Apostolov for pointing it out.
- **Clearer English labels and shorter hints.** Settings, Wiki, Memory, Skills, and related screens use more explicit actions and consistent terms. Hints repeat less information and fit more comfortably beside their controls; privacy and deletion warnings keep their consequences. (#409) Thanks to Apostol Apostolov.

### Fixed

- **The steer field is as wide as the chain.** The pill opened a 520 px field inside a 583 px chain, and its box ran to the edge of the message body instead of stopping with the chain. The field now runs from where the steps' text starts to where the chain ends, with the pill on the steps' rail, in a wide or a narrow window, without sideways scrolling.
- **A DM's settings show where the agent answers again.** Since teams came in, the "Conversation with …" window (from a DM's menu in the sidebar, or the gear on the agent's card) showed only Cancel and Save. It shows the choice between answers in the conversation and answers in threads again.
- **In English, the Vault is the Vault.** With the app in English, the sidebar, the search, the Vault page, an agent's page, the step that used a key, the activity log and the card that asks for a key all said "Cofre". They say "Vault" now, and so do the errors and the outcome a person reads when a key is missing or saved. The few other spots that stayed in Portuguese in English are English too: the dictation shortcut ("⌥ Space"), the placeholder for a Whisper model's path, "Invalid URL" on a screenshot and "Error" on a connector's sign-in page. A test now reads the screen's code and fails on Portuguese in an English string, or on Portuguese shown with no English beside it.

## [0.2.44] - 2026-11-21

> **Em português:**
> - **Novo:** o **Cofre**, no pé da barra lateral. Guarda chaves de API e logins de sites que os agentes usam sem nunca ver. Uma chave entra só no comando que a pede e aparece como `[cofre:NOME]` no que ele imprime. Um login é digitado pelo app só no site exato. Cada chave pode pedir aprovação a cada uso. Num time, há as do time e as suas. Tem importação de `.env`, e cada uso fica no registro.
> - **Novo:** quando falta uma chave ou um login, o agente pede no chat. Qualquer pessoa da conversa cola no card, e vai direto pro Cofre, sem passar pela conversa. Os logins salvos na página do agente passam pro Cofre.
> - **Novo:** o seu **harness** (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor…) monta o Tucano e conversa com os agentes **por MCP**. Fica em Configurações › Harnesses.
> - **Novo:** um agente arquivado pode ser **excluído de vez**. O que ele escreveu fica na conversa com o nome riscado, como no Slack.
> - **Mudou:** os agentes **levam um pedido até o fim** sozinhos, chamam uns aos outros e chamam um colega que pode ajudar. Param quando não andam mais, com um aviso e **Continuar**. O `/objetivo` saiu: agora todo pedido funciona assim.
> - **Mudou:** uma resposta que falha **tenta de novo sozinha**, e o trabalho cortado ao fechar o app sempre continua.
> - **Corrigido:** em Uso e limites, uma janela da assinatura que já virou aparece vazia, não cheia.

### Added

- **The Cofre: keys the agents use by name, without ever seeing them.** A new item under **Connectors** at the foot of the sidebar keeps API keys and tokens (`STRIPE_KEY`, `OPENAI_API_KEY`), encrypted in the system keychain or as a 1Password reference, and never shows a value again, only its last characters. An agent with a computer asks for a key by name in a command (`bash` or `start_service` with `keys`): Tucano checks it was given to that agent, puts the value in that command's environment only, and turns it into `[cofre:NAME]` in what the command prints before the agent reads it. The step shows which key the command used. A new key is given to no agent until you pick them, on the key or on the agent's page (**Tools › Cofre keys**). A key can ask for approval on every use, which holds the command like a commit would. In a team, the team's keys are the admins' to make and give out, and each person has their own, which no one else sees and an agent uses only in work that person asked for. **Import .env** reads a pasted file and shows each line before saving. A connector's variable can point to a team key with `cofre:NAME`. The **Log** tab lists every use (key, agent, for whom, where), never a value.
- **Site logins live in the Cofre, and an agent asks for a missing key or login in the chat.** The **Logins** tab keeps a site, a username and a password (or a 1Password reference), in the same two drawers as the keys, given to agents one by one: the app types it into the agent's browser only on that exact site, and each sign-in goes in the log. The logins saved on an agent's page move into the Cofre as the team's, given to the same agents, and the agent's page now lists the Cofre's logins under its browser, with **Save a login…**. When the work needs a key or a login the agent doesn't have, it asks with `pedir_chave`: a card shows in the conversation with the key's name (or the site) and why. Anyone in the conversation pastes it there, straight into the Cofre (their own, or the team's when an admin ticks **Keep it for the team**), and the agent picks up where it stopped. A key already in the Cofre is offered as **Give it to …** instead (the team's, by an admin). The value never goes through the conversation or the agent.

- **Your own harness can now set Tucano up and talk to its agents, over MCP.** Tucano is now an MCP server too: connect Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, OpenCode or any MCP client, and from there it sees the team, sends a message to an agent and waits for the answer, and creates or changes agents, channels, routines, skills, connectors and memories, as you would on the screens. A common first use: "create in Tucano the equivalent of the agents and skills you have". On the desktop, **Settings › Harnesses › Connect in one click** writes the connection into the harness's own configuration; anywhere, **Make a key** gives a key, shown once, with what to paste into each harness. Each key acts as the person who made it and goes only as far as its level (read, talk or configure). Nothing over MCP deletes anything, decides an approval, touches people, logins or secrets, or loosens a tool's approval. A memory a harness sends waits for you to accept it, and what a harness created or changed shows in **Approvals**. On the desktop it answers only programs on the same computer; on a server, at `/mcp` on its address.
- **An archived agent can be deleted for good.** Until now an agent could only be archived, so one you'd never use again stayed in the "Archived" list forever. An archived agent now has **Delete for good** on its page and a trash button in "Archived", confirmed by typing its name. Like a deactivated person in Slack, what it wrote stays in the conversations under its name, greyed and struck out, its receipts stay in Usage and limits, and a DM with it stays to read (with no message box). Its instructions, permissions, own memories, routines, services, channels, computer, browser logins and folder go, agents that answered to it answer to no one, and its @handle is free for a new agent. A deleted agent can't be reactivated or edited.

### Changed

- **Agents carry a request through to the end on their own.** A message now sets off work that goes on until a reply hands nothing to anyone and waits on nothing. There's no longer a cap of a few replies in a row. It stops by itself when the work stops moving, with a note in the conversation saying why, what got done and where it stopped, and **Continuar** to go on. That happens after 3 replies in a row that changed nothing, the same reply or the same request to the same agent 3 times, 2 hours with nothing new, or 100 replies. `/objetivo` is gone, since every request now works this way, and so is the setting for replies in a row.
- **Agents call each other in.** An agent can ask any other agent for help, not only the ones near it in the org chart. A request carries the original ask, how the work was split and what's been delivered. After a reply that hands nothing on, the router may call in one other agent of the channel that has something concrete to add, shown as "**X** came in: why", at most 3 times per request. This can be turned off per agent (on its page) or per channel (in its menu). A message that asks nothing of anyone is now left unanswered.
- **A reply that fails tries again by itself, and work the app's closing cut off always resumes.** A failure that passes (a limit, a dropped connection) waits and retries on its own after 5, 10, 20 and then 30 minutes. Interrupted work resumes however long ago it stopped, told to read what changed and to check any outward action it isn't sure went through. A reply that stops at its per-reply limit while making progress goes on by itself up to 10 times instead of 2.

### Fixed

- **Usage and limits no longer shows a subscription window that already reset as full.** Claude Code and Codex report their windows only when a reply runs, so a report of 100% taken just before the five hours ended stayed on the page as "100% used, resets now" until the next reply, while claude.ai already showed 0%. A window whose reset time has passed now shows 0% and when it reset, and a page left open redraws when the window resets.

> **Em português:**
> - **Corrigido:** numa resposta, os passos mostram uma linha **Pensando**, não duas, quando o agente roda ferramentas ao mesmo tempo.
> - **Corrigido:** um **card** que um agente propõe (time, agente, canal, rotina) é aplicado uma vez só: não volta pra **Criar** enquanto a resposta continua, e um segundo clique não cria os agentes de novo.
> - **Corrigido:** na barra lateral, os agentes **arquivados** ficam num grupo que começa fechado, e o notebook na linha de um agente não pula mais.
> - **Corrigido:** numa **wiki** em inglês, uma página nova não abre mais com uma linha em negrito "tipo: … criada: … atualizada: …". As propriedades da página ficam no idioma do Tucano (`type`, `created`, `updated` em inglês), e um bloco de propriedades que o curador escreva no texto vira propriedade, não texto.
> - **Corrigido:** os **arquivos** que os agentes mandam podem ser baixados, também num servidor (Tucano Pro), e salvos com **Salvar…** no desktop.

### Fixed

- **A reply's steps show one "Thinking" row, not two.** Engines run tool calls side by side (Pi, Codex and Claude Code all do). The second call to start closed the first one's row as "Thought", hiding the command it was running, and each call added its own "Thinking" row when it ended, so two rows spun and stopped together. A tool starting now closes only the "Thinking" row, and the row comes back once the last call running beside it ends.
- **A card an agent proposes is applied once.** A team, agent, channel or routine card applied while the agent's reply was still working went back to **Create team** (or **Create**, **Apply**) as the reply went on, because the reply saved its own copy of the message over the card; a second click made the same agents again. The reply now keeps what was done with its cards, an agent proposing the same team again in that reply is told it already exists, and the core refuses to apply a card twice even if its message lost that it was applied (and puts the card back as it was). A reply tried again keeps the cards that were already applied.
- **Archived agents fold away under "Archived" in the sidebar.** An archived agent with a DM was drawn above the "Archived" entry, mixed with the last sector's agents, and couldn't be hidden. "Archived" is now a group like a sector: closed until you open it, remembered when you do, with its agents below it (each opens its DM, or its page when there's none) and "Reactivate…" at the end.
- **The laptop on an agent's row stays in one place.** It sat before the working dots, the unread count and the gear, so it jumped left whenever the agent started working or you hovered the row. It's now the row's last item, with the gear's room kept, and what comes and goes shows before it.
- **A wiki page in an English workspace no longer opens with "tipo: … criada: … atualizada: …" in bold.** Tucano wrote a page's frontmatter keys in Portuguese whatever the language, and the curator, having seen them, wrote that block into a new page's text, where it rendered as a rule and a heading. The keys now follow the workspace's language (`type`, `created`, `updated` in English; a page that has them in the other language keeps its own), a frontmatter block at the top of a page's text is taken out of the text and into the page's properties, and the curator is told its text is the body only.
- **Files the agents send can be saved, on a server too.** A card for a file in a reply only had Open, and in a browser a .docx (or anything a browser can't show) opened an empty panel, so on Tucano Pro there was no way to get it. Every file and image an agent sends now has **Download** in the browser (on the card, in the panel and in the image viewer), served by the server under the file's real name to whoever can see the conversation; a file a browser can't show downloads on a click. On the desktop, Open stays and **Save…** sits beside it.

## [0.2.41] - 2026-11-19

> **Em português:**
> - **Novo:** **modo objetivo**. Escreva `/objetivo …` num canal e os agentes trabalham nele sozinhos, passando a vez entre si (faz, revisa, corrige, revisa de novo), até um deles marcar como cumprido. Para sozinho, com um aviso no canal, em 12 respostas, se o time andar em círculos, se ninguém passar a vez ou se uma resposta falhar.
> - **Novo:** **Continuar sozinho**. Uma resposta que pausa no limite por resposta e estava avançando segue sozinha, até 2 vezes seguidas, se o mês ainda comporta. Quando o app reabre, as respostas cortadas pelo fechamento continuam sozinhas. A chave fica em Configurações › Agentes › Limites.
> - **Novo:** o navegador do agente **baixa arquivos** e **manda no chat**, e entra em sites com **logins salvos** (na página do agente, só admin), sem o modelo ver a senha. Códigos de verificação ele lê no e-mail conectado ou pergunta pra você.
> - **Mudou:** o que o **Tuca** propõe e só mexe dentro do app (agentes, times, canais, rotinas) **entra na hora, com Desfazer** por 24 horas. Acesso a conectores e limite do mês continuam esperando você confirmar.
> - **Mudou:** agentes fora de qualquer time e sem chefe **trabalham juntos** sem organograma, e vários chamados na mesma mensagem **respondem um depois do outro**, cada um lendo o que os anteriores disseram. Eles também perguntam menos: fazem o que dá pra decidir e, quando perguntam, é uma pergunta só, com a recomendação deles.
> - **Mudou:** o navegador do agente só aparece grande no chat enquanto tem algo pra ver.
> - **Corrigido:** uma pergunta de um pedido que chegou até você fecha onde quer que você responda, e dá pra acrescentar algo a um pedido ainda em andamento.

### Added

- **Goals: `/objetivo …` (or `/goal …`) in a channel.** The agents there work on it by themselves, passing the turn to each other with @ (build, review, fix, review again), past the usual replies in a row, until one of them calls `concluir_objetivo` with the result. Each reply is told the goal and how many replies are left. It stops by itself, with a line in the channel saying why, at 12 replies, when an agent says the same as before, when a reply neither passes the turn nor ends it, or when a reply fails, is stopped or hits its limit. A goal lives in memory: if the app closes, it ends there. (#556)
- **Keep going on its own.** A reply that pauses at its per-reply limit while getting somewhere (a tool call went through) goes on by itself, up to 2 times in a row, when the month still has room for another reply that size; after that it waits for **Continue**. When the app opens again, replies cut off by it closing go on by themselves, unless the workspace is paused (they wait for it to resume) or it was more than 12 hours ago. Settings › Agents › Limits › **Keep going on its own**, on by default. (#556, part of #389)
- **An agent's browser downloads files, posts them in the chat and signs in.** Downloads go to a folder of the agent's own, up to 20 MB each; `navegador_anexar` posts one in the conversation. An admin saves **Saved logins** on the agent's page, kept in the system keychain (or as a 1Password reference): `navegador_login` fills them in without the model ever seeing the password, only on the exact address saved and only over HTTPS (http just on localhost), and the password is masked in snapshots and screenshots. For a verification code, the agent reads the connected email or asks you. (#556)

### Changed

- **Tuca's changes inside the app go in at once, with Undo.** What Tuca proposes for agents, teams, channels and routines, or an agent's name, role, instructions, model, effort and web access, applies as soon as Tuca finishes replying, with the same checks as before, and its card gets **Undo** for 24 hours. Undo says why when it's no longer safe (the agent already worked, it was edited since, the channel has messages). Connector access, the monthly limit and anything the person couldn't apply themselves still wait for **Apply**. Settings › Agents › Tuca turns it off. (#556)
- **Agents work together without an org chart.** Agents in no team and with no lead know each other as colleagues and can ask each other with `pedir`, with no setup. Several agents called in one message, or routed to one, answer one after another, each reading what the ones before said. Agents are told to do what they can decide instead of asking, and to ask one question, with their recommendation, when they must. (#554)
- **An agent's browser is big in the chat only while there's something to watch:** while the reply using it is working or waiting on an approval, or while a person has taken over. Otherwise it's the small card, which opens the browser on the side. (#552)
- **More for a request still under way.** `pedir` with `continuar` adds to a request that's still being worked, in its thread, instead of opening a second one. (#551)

### Fixed

- **A question taken up from a request closes wherever it's answered.** Answering it in the request's thread now also closes the one the agent took to the person, so it no longer stays open for good with a second badge in the sidebar. (#551)

> - **Mudou:** o harness do Gemini CLI virou o do **Antigravity CLI** (`agy`), o CLI de agente do Google, com os modelos Gemini 4 e 3.8 e também Claude e GPT-OSS pela mesma conta Google. Quem usava o Gemini passa pro Antigravity sem refazer nada: os agentes, os modelos e os recibos vêm junto. Cada resposta roda isolada, sem acesso ao terminal, aos seus arquivos nem às suas configurações do `agy`, e só com as ferramentas do Tucano. Obrigado, @Vialc!
> - **Correções:** um print anexado não vai mais pro modelo a cada resposta: ele vai só na mensagem em que você mandou (depois o agente sabe o nome e, se precisar, olha de novo), o que acaba com o agente comentando um "print novo" e gastando tokens à toa; e um conector do Google sem login válido mostra **Autorizar** em vez de "conectado", e uma chamada recusada diz o motivo (falta login, e-mail fora dos usuários de teste, permissão faltando, API não ativada ou Developer Preview).

### Changed

- **The Gemini CLI harness is now the Antigravity CLI (`agy`).** Google's agent CLI runs Gemini 4 and 3.8, and Claude and GPT-OSS too, on the same Google account; it's detected by `agy models`, which also says whether it's signed in (the login is in the keychain, not a file). What ran on the "gemini" harness moves over by itself: the harness switched on, agents' models and fallbacks, and receipts; an old Gemini CLI model name runs as the Antigravity one it became. Each turn runs in a folder of its own with a home of its own, so none of the person's `agy` settings, MCP servers, rules, skills, plugins or hooks come in: its settings deny the CLI's own shell, file reads and writes, page reads (unless the agent has the web) and browser, and allow only the gateway's tools. A denied tool comes back to the model as an error and the reply goes on. The agent's instructions are the workspace's rules file, or the top of the prompt when they're over its 24 KB limit. On macOS the turn's home links in only the keychain. `search_web` has no permission of its own and stays on; it shows as a web search, so what it brings in counts as content from outside. Images can't go along (the CLI takes text only), and the agent is told. Thanks, @Vialc ([#158](https://github.com/eduwxyz/tucano/pull/158)).

### Fixed

- **An attached image goes to the model only in its own turn.** Every turn that sent the whole conversation (every turn on engines without kept sessions, and on Claude Code whenever its session starts over) also sent the newest six images in it, after they were already answered, so the model kept "seeing" a screenshot the person hadn't just sent, commented on it again and spent about 1.5k input tokens per image per turn. Now an image goes as an image only until this agent has answered it; later turns keep `[imagem anexada: name]`, and a new `ver_imagem` tool lets the agent look at an earlier attachment again when it needs to. An agent on this computer is also told not to open or list the person's Desktop, Downloads, screenshots or photos unless asked. Pasting text that comes with a picture of itself (a spreadsheet cell, a page) pastes the text instead of attaching the picture.
- **A Google connector without a working sign-in asks to authorize.** Google's MCP servers answer `initialize` and `tools/list` without a token and refuse only `tools/call`, so a connector with no usable sign-in showed as connected, its first tool call failed with a bare "Unauthorized", and reconnecting didn't help. A connector that signs in with the person's own app (Google, Slack) and has no usable sign-in now shows **Authorize**, which opens the provider's sign-in. A call turned away says why: missing sign-in, refused even after renewing (check Test users and the AI-agent client), a missing permission, an API not enabled (with the link to enable it) or the Developer Preview. A token Google refused is not renewed again on the next Authorize; it signs in from scratch. The guide's Google troubleshooting covers each message.

## [0.2.20] - 2026-11-08

> **Em português:**
> - **A wiki sincronizada com um repositório git.** Em Ajustes da wiki › A pasta › Sincronizar com git, cole o endereço do repositório (o seu cofre do Obsidian no GitHub, por exemplo), adicione a chave que o Tucano gera como chave de deploy, e teste. A wiki vira um clone dele: traz o que mudou a cada 2 minutos e envia cada página salva uns segundos depois. Num conflito, fica a versão do repositório e a do Tucano vira "(versão do Tucano)", com um aviso em Cuidados. Uma chave sem escrita deixa a wiki só leitura. Páginas guardadas pra uma pessoa e fontes de DM nunca vão pro repositório.

### Added

- **Git sync for the wiki's folder.** A Tucano on a server (or in the cloud) can now use the person's Obsidian vault kept in a git repository as the wiki's source of truth. In Wiki settings › The folder › **Sync with git**, the owner or an admin pastes the repository's SSH address (an https one is turned into it), and Tucano makes an ed25519 key for this wiki, kept sealed in the app's secrets and never written to a log. The public key comes with **Copy**, a link to the host's add-deploy-key page (GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket, Gitea and Codeberg), and the steps: paste it, tick "Allow write access" to read and write. **Test the connection** runs `git ls-remote` with the key and clones the repository apart, counting its pages, PDFs and docx. Connecting makes the clone the wiki's folder (in the app's data); the folder the wiki had stays as it was, as a backup. A wiki that already has pages either **uses the repository** as is or **merges** them in, where a page with the same name and other text comes in as "<name> (versão do Tucano)" and held pages stay held. From then on Tucano pulls on start, every 2 minutes and before the curator writes, and commits and pushes a few seconds after a page is saved, as "Tucano: <page> (<who>)". A conflict is rebased: the repository's version stays in place, Tucano's is committed next to it as "(versão do Tucano)", and a notice shows in **Care**. The pages held for someone after the first invite, sources from a DM, Tucano's own `índice.md` and `registro.md`, and files over 50 MB are never committed (kept out by the commit's path filter and `.git/info/exclude`). Network and key errors show in Wiki settings with the last sync, **Sync now** and retries with backoff; a revoked key says so. A key that can't push is found on the first push: the wiki turns **read-only** (a badge, and no writes by anyone, the curator included), what didn't go stays in the folder, and **Try writing again** checks once write access is on. SSH runs with the key in a temporary 0600 file, `IdentitiesOnly`, `StrictHostKeyChecking=accept-new` and a `known_hosts` of the app's own. Needs git on the computer; Tucano says so plainly when it's missing. New core calls, for the owner and admins: `wikiGitStatus`, `wikiGitSetup`, `wikiGitTest`, `wikiGitConnect`, `wikiGitSync`, `wikiGitDisconnect`. The Tuca guide has a new **wiki-git** page, Obsidian Git on the Mac included.

### Added

- **Sign a harness in on a server from the browser or the phone, with no SSH.** On Tucano as a server, **Sign in** (Settings › Models and the accounts) used to show a command to run in the server's terminal. Now the server runs the harness's own login in a terminal of its own (through `script`, on Linux) and the browser shows it in a window: the screen as text, **Open the sign-in page**, the code to type there with a copy button, a field to paste a code back, and arrows, Enter and Esc for a menu. Each harness signs in the way that works with no browser on the server: Codex and Grok with a device code, Claude Code by pasting the code its page shows, Cursor by approving its page, OpenCode and Gemini in their own menus, Pi with `/login`. The window closes the login once the harness checks as signed in, and a login left open ends after 15 minutes. Tucano passes keys and shows the screen; the harness saves the login where it always does. The iPhone app shows the same window (in its next version). On a desktop, **Sign in** opens a terminal as before.
- **Notifications on the iPhone with the app closed.** Approvals, questions and finished replies used to reach the Tucano app only while it was open. Now a phone that allows notifications gives its Tucano (a server, or the computer at home) a handle, and the Tucano asks a relay at tucano.sh to deliver through Apple. The handle is the phone's Apple token sealed by the relay, which alone holds the app's push key: a Tucano can reach only the phones that connected to it, and a disconnected device stops getting them. The relay passes on the title and the line, as the desktop's notification shows them, and stores nothing. `TUCANO_PUSH_URL` points at another relay, or `off`. Tapping a notification opens its conversation, on the right Tucano (in the next version of the app).
- **The connector store on the iPhone.** The phone app could only add a connector by its MCP address. Now it gets the same store as the computer (48 services, by category, found by name or by what they do) from the Tucano itself, so the list is always the one that Tucano can connect: a tap connects, and a service that signs in with its account opens its page right there; one that asks something first (a token, your own instance's address) or warns that the data goes through a third party shows a short form; Google's and Slack's connectors walk through making your own app, with a link per step. New core calls: `connectorCatalog`, `addCatalogConnector` (the core fills the entry in, as the desktop's form does) and `ownAppGuide`. The computer's own-app window now reads the same steps (in the iPhone app's next version).

### Fixed

- **The iPhone connects to the computer's Tucano on a real home network.** The QR code in Settings › Phone › Your iPhone named the Mac by its Bonjour name (`name.local`), which the iPhone app couldn't reach on a real Wi-Fi: it waited and gave up with "Couldn't reach your Tucano", while the same QR code with the address connected. It now carries the computer's address on the home network, read each time a QR code is made, as on Windows and Linux.
- **An agent with GitHub on "Ler e escrever" pushes and opens the PR instead of saying the session is read-only.** On Codex, the CLI's own text about its sandbox ("`sandbox_mode` is `read-only`... Network access is restricted", "Approval policy is never") reached the model, and it answered "Estou bloqueado porque esta sessão permite apenas leitura" for a push or a PR it had every grant for. That text is left out now (in `codex exec` and the app-server), and the note an agent with the app's tools gets says Codex's sandbox doesn't limit them, that git and other commands on its computer run with `bash`, and to say exactly what was refused or waits for approval, never "read-only". On every harness, an agent with a GitHub connector gets a **GitHub** section in its instructions, made from what it really has: the tools it publishes with (`create_branch`, `push_files`, `create_or_update_file`, `create_pull_request`, by the names it sees); on **Este computador**, that `git push` also runs with your own git credentials; on **Linux isolado**, that there are none there; what your autonomy level or rules make ask first or turn off; and, with read-only access, to ask for read and write with `pedir_acesso`. Before, an agent on this computer was told only "git push only when asked", with no word about the connector.
- **The agent page's Computador card shows what each choice means, from the first look.** Clicking **Este computador** after **Linux isolado** showed the confirmation on top of the Linux panel (the Docker state, **Pastas do seu computador**, **Apagar computador**), with Linux still picked and already saved, and that mix read as this computer's full card, while the real one looked cut short. Now the confirmation shows alone, with **Este computador** picked, and **Cancelar** (or clicking the saved choice) goes back to it; once allowed, the card says the commands run on your computer as you, starting in its folder, and that it reaches your folders without lending them and needs no Docker.
- **OAuth connectors stay signed in across updates and long sessions.** A server that rotates refresh tokens takes each one once. When two requests found the access token expired together (two tool calls, or a call and the event stream), both renewed with the same refresh token: one got the new sign-in and saved it, the other got `invalid_grant`, and the MCP SDK deleted the saved sign-in, the new one included. The connector then asked for authorization at the next launch, which for an app left open is usually the update's. Now a renewal goes to the server once per refresh token and every request waiting on it gets the same answer, and a refusal deletes only the sign-in that was refused. A token endpoint that doesn't answer (a network error, a 5xx, a proxy's page) no longer reads as "Precisa autorizar": the connector says the renewal failed and tries again by itself. **Autorizar no navegador** first renews the saved sign-in, without the browser; before, it always registered the app again and threw the saved sign-in away. And a saved value the system's key won't open (the Keychain said no) is never deleted: the connector says so, with what to do, and doesn't retry; **Autorizar no navegador** then signs in again in its place.

If Claude Code, Codex or another one is already signed in, it shows up tested with a real reply (and how long it took), with switches to bring the tools and skills it found along (**ver quais** names them), and the two small models behind the scenes picked on their own (**Trocar** shows them as two cards, who replies and the memory, each opening one menu with every ready harness's models). It starts safe: no computer, connectors read-only, the web only where the goal needs it; a connector that fits the goal and signs in with one click in the browser is offered as optional (Todoist for the week, Notion for writing, Linear for code; none for the others, and nothing that needs an app, a token or an address of your own). **Ajustar com o Tuca** talks it over with him and brings his card back; **Prefiro configurar do zero** opens the detailed form. **Chamar … pro time** lands in #geral with the first message written and not sent, and a balloon over the field says to press send.
